Data visualizations influence how people understand communities and outcomes. Thoughtful design helps avoid harm, bias, and exclusion. It also makes data easier to understand and use, including by communities represented in the data.

## Be empathetic

* Consider how the data, framing, and presentation may affect different communities. 
* Avoid representing groups in ways that could cause harm or reinforce stereotypes.

## Protect people’s privacy and dignity

* Avoid exposing private and sensitive information.
* Ensure people can’t connect data points to figure out who someone is.

## Represent people respectfully and inclusively 

* Choose language, categories, colors, and visuals that reflect people accurately and respectfully. 
* Avoid stereotypes and generalizations. For example, avoid using pink to represent women and blue for men.

## Make inequities visible with care

* Provide context when showing disparities to avoid misunderstanding.
* Show the disparity, but don’t use a deficit narrative. This is when differences are attributed to the characteristics of a group. Instead, highlight the systems or root causes that create the disparity.

## Support translation and adaptability

* Create labels and descriptions you can translate and reuse in different languages.
