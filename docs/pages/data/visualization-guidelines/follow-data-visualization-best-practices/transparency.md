Design visualizations so they help people understand and trust the data.

## Be clear about limitations

Acknowledge uncertainty, gaps, or constraints in the data.

## Provide enough context to interpret the data

* Define terms or categories that may be unfamiliar to others.
* Provide a data dictionary if you are allowing people to download the raw data. For more information on data dictionaries, visit [Creating Metadata and a Data Dictionary](https://data.ca.gov/pages/creating-metadata-and-data-dictionary).
* Include the date the data was last updated.

## Share methodology and sources

* Clearly identify sources and ownership.
* Include the data source in the subtitle or below the chart.
* Include methodology for how data was collected, cleaned, analyzed or modeled. Give enough information so others can understand how you produced your results.

## Make data downloadable 

* Provide access to the underlying data, when possible.
* Protect confidential and sensitive information.
* When possible, add links in accessible formats such as a CSV, Excel or JSON file.
